What does the business get from this?

A daily list that helps turn "just looking" into sales, and stops most reminders to people who already bought. The CRM manager (who runs customer messages) uses it to pick tomorrow's reminders.

Full rollout, per month, in USD. Revenue is not profit: margin takes off the cost of goods and the cost of sending. Only the 25% comes from this demo; every other input is an assumption.

How we'd prove it, then decide

  1. Split once, at random. When someone joins the list, a coin flip puts them in the reminder group (9 in 10) or the holdout (1 in 10) for the whole test.
  2. One reminder each per product during the test.
  3. Same 14 days. Count each person's purchases of that product, online and at the tills, in the 14 days after joining.

  • Expand if margin after costs is above $0, the gap's 90% range is above 0, and unsubscribes rise under 0.2 points.
  • Keep testing if the gap looks positive but its 90% range still includes 0.

1

Reporting

Who should get tomorrow's reminder, and how many people is that?

The daily list, plus one shared count, "viewed but not bought": 6 people today. Everyone reads the same number.

2

Process modelling

How do we measure "look, then buy" when people switch between the website and the shops?

Model: written rules, one name tag per person, and a 7-day look-back. Measures: the look-to-buy rate, and the share of shop sales linked to a person (75%).

3

Diagnosis

Why do people look but not buy?

  1. Look: 8 people looked and had no online purchase.
  2. Guess: some bought in a shop. Test: add the till sales. Found: 2 of 8 did, so 6 are left.
  3. New guess: for those 6, the price puts them off. Test (proposed): show half of them a 5% offer and compare with the other half.
  4. Act: 3 people per group is far too few, so test on a large, similar audience. Keep the offer only if its extra margin, after the discount, beats no offer. A win shows the offer changes buying; it does not prove price was the first reason. If not, check stock and photos.
4

Prediction

Will reminders earn more than they cost?

  1. Forecast: 2 extra buyers per 100 reminders → $41,700 extra margin a month (assumed inputs).
  2. Test: the 14-day random split.
  3. Decide: expand, keep testing, change or stop, using the rule after the test.
  4. Learn: put the measured gap back into the calculator, so the next forecast is better.

What I did first, and what I skipped

    Positions are my judgement. Impact means how much an item changes who gets a reminder, or how sure we are that reminders pay. Effort means rough build time.

    Honest limits

    • Made-up data: 12 shoppers and 1 sofa.
    • The SQL and YAML were not run on a real warehouse or through dbt. The page's JavaScript follows the same rules, and its counts are what you see. The metric file is a concept: it works on daily rows, so its match with the list is not verified.
    • The list is a candidate list. Opt-outs, staff and bots are removed by an eligibility check; the made-up data has none of them.
    • Exact keys can still be wrong, for example a family sharing one phone. Keys shared by two people are left unlinked.
    • The money numbers are forecasts from assumed inputs, not results.
    • The review score is one AI's judgement against its own rubric, not a certificate.
